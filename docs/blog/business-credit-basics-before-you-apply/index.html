Business Credit Basics: 7 Steps Before You Apply for a Loan

By Yolanda "Yo" Butler · November 4, 2026

Before applying for a business loan, separate your personal and business finances, make your business official, build a record of on-time payments, and gather the documents lenders ask for. Starting three to six months early gives you time to fix problems instead of explaining them.

This article is education, not financial or legal advice. For your specific situation, talk with a licensed professional or a trusted lender.

Why does business credit matter?

Business credit helps lenders, suppliers and partners see whether your company pays its bills reliably. A strong profile can open better terms and protect your personal credit from carrying the whole business. During my years at Operation HOPE, the owners who prepared early walked into the bank with confidence instead of dread.

What are the seven steps?

  • Separate your money: open a business bank account and run every business dollar through it.
  • Make it official: register your business properly and get an employer identification number (EIN).
  • Use consistent details: the same business name, address and phone number everywhere.
  • Start small with vendors: some suppliers offer terms and report payments, which helps build history.
  • Pay early or on time, every time: payment history matters more than almost anything else.
  • Keep clean books: a simple monthly profit and loss statement and bank reconciliation.
  • Check your personal credit too: many small business lenders still look at it.

Which documents do lenders ask for?

Requirements vary, but most lenders ask for some combination of business and personal tax returns, recent bank statements, a profit and loss statement, a balance sheet, your business registration and a short explanation of how much you need and how you will repay it.

How do you tell your funding story?

Numbers open the door, but your story keeps it open. Be ready to explain in two minutes what your business does, who pays you, what the money is for and how it will help you repay. Practice it out loud. Confidence is built by preparation, not by luck.

"Lenders are not looking for perfect. They are looking for prepared."
